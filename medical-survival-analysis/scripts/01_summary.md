# Procedure 01: Baseline Characteristics Summary & Interpretation

## Objective
To describe and analyze the baseline demographic, clinical, and laboratory characteristics of 299 heart failure patients, stratified by clinical outcome (`Deceased` vs. `Survived`). This analysis identifies key clinical variables significantly associated with patient mortality.

## Cohort Overview
* **Total Sample Size (n):** 299 patients.
* **Mortality Rate:** 32.1% (96 patients deceased, 203 patients survived).
* **Data Integrity:** 0% missing data across all analyzed clinical variables.

## Key Statistical Findings & Clinical Insights

### 1. Demographics & Age Analytics
* **Finding:** Deceased patients were significantly older (Mean = 65.2 years, SD = 13.2) compared to survivors (Mean = 58.8 years, SD = 10.6).
* **Statistical Significance:** Highly significant ($p < 0.001$, Welch's T-test).
* **Clinical Insight:** Advanced age remains a non-modifiable, dominant risk factor driving mortality rates in heart failure cohorts.

### 2. Cardiac & Renal Pathophysiology Indicators
* **Ejection Fraction (%):**
  * *Finding:* Deceased patients exhibited a significantly lower mean ejection fraction (33.5%, SD = 12.5) than survivors (40.3%, SD = 10.9).
  * *Significance:* Highly significant ($p < 0.001$, Welch's T-test).
  * *Clinical Insight:* Poor cardiac pumping efficiency strongly correlates with fatal outcomes.
* **Serum Creatinine (mg/dL):**
  * *Finding:* Deceased patients had higher median serum creatinine levels (1.3 mg/dL [IQR: 1.1, 1.9]) compared to survivors (1.0 mg/dL [IQR: 0.9, 1.2]).
  * *Significance:* Highly significant ($p < 0.001$, Kruskal-Wallis test).
  * *Clinical Insight:* Elevated creatinine indicates impaired kidney function, underscoring cardiorenal syndrome as a severe risk factor for mortality.

### 3. Electrolyte Imbalance
* **Serum Sodium (mEq/L):**
  * *Finding:* Deceased patients presented lower mean serum sodium levels (135.4 mEq/L) compared to survivors (137.2 mEq/L).
  * *Significance:* Statistically significant ($p = 0.002$, Welch's T-test).
  * *Clinical Insight:* Hyponatremia (low sodium levels) is a known adverse marker in heart failure management.

### 4. Non-Significant Co-morbidities & Demographics
* Diabetes Mellitus ($p = 1.000$), Hypertension ($p = 0.214$), Anaemia ($p = 0.307$), Smoking Status ($p = 0.932$), and Sex ($p = 1.000$) did not demonstrate statistically significant univariate differences between survivors and deceased patients in this baseline cohort.
